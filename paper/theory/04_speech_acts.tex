\section{What cannot be decided from text}\label{sec:speech}

\begin{theorem}[No unconditional soundness without the customer]\label{thm:nomodel}
Under Assumption~\ref{as:adv}(b), let a view-measurable system execute $o$ without asking on some
view $\view(h_0)$. Then there is a history $h_1$ with $\view(h_1) = \view(h_0)$ and
$\Perm(h_1, o) = 0$. Hence a system is sound on all histories if and only if it never executes
without the customer approving the operation through the interface.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $h_1$ be $h_0$ with every customer message's voice set to $\quoted$, which the assumption
permits. Then $\Own(h_1) = \emptyset$, so $\Out_a(h_1) = \emptyset$ and $\Perm(h_1, o) = 0$. The
view is unchanged, so the system acts as on $h_0$ (with the same probability, if randomised). For
the converse, an approval of $o$ through the interface is an own-voice request naming every
argument of $o$. It cannot be quoted (Assumption~\ref{as:adv}(c)), and it makes
$\Perm = 1$.
\end{proof}

The fixed interface is the one place where the customer's voice is structural. Everything else the
customer types is text, and whose words it is remains a reading of that text.

\begin{corollary}[Where $G$ depends on a model]\label{cor:residual}
$G$ executes without the customer exactly when every piece is $\Au$-backed, whether or not the
agent proposed it (a proposal is bound only if an $\Au$ record or request carries it). Its soundness then
rests on the faithfulness and completeness of the customer's records (Theorem~\ref{thm:sound}),
that is, on $Q$ (voice), $R$ and $L$ (which requests were made), and $E$ (which values were
asserted). All four are readings of customer text; the ceiling makes every other channel
model-free. A variant of $G$ that always asks for the final approval would be sound on every
history and independent of every model, at the price of one approval per executed operation.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proposition}[The unavoidable error of reading the voice]\label{prop:bayes}
Let histories be drawn from a distribution, and for a customer text $x$ let
$\pi(x) = \Pr[\omega = \own \mid x]$. Every classifier $c$ from text to $\{\own, \quoted\}$
satisfies, for any weights $\alpha, \beta > 0$,
\[
\alpha \Pr[c = \own,\ \omega = \quoted] + \beta \Pr[c = \quoted,\ \omega = \own]
\;\geq\; \mathbb{E}_x \min\big(\alpha (1 - \pi(x)),\ \beta\, \pi(x)\big),
\]
with equality for the Bayes rule. The bound is zero if and only if the voice is almost surely a
function of the text.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Conditional on $x$, the classifier pays $\alpha(1 - \pi(x))$ by answering $\own$ and
$\beta\pi(x)$ by answering $\quoted$; it can do no better than the smaller of the two.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The first term is the part of ASR due to a false $\Au$. The second is TSR lost; it is no more
than that because the gate treats lowered customer words as a veto
(Proposition~\ref{prop:lower}). The benchmark's Q2D
pairs mark their quotations in the text, so for them $\pi \in \{0, 1\}$ and the bound is zero. A
Q2D failure measured there is therefore a classifier's error, not a necessary one. An unmarked
paste (Example~\ref{ex:voice}) has $0 < \pi < 1$, and there every reader of text errs. The same
argument applies to $R$ and $L$ wherever what the customer asks for is genuinely ambiguous.
\end{remark}
