\section{Open questions}\label{sec:open}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Time and revocation.} $\Perm$ has no time. Adding expiry and revocation (EAL-Bench,
  arXiv:2609.01836) makes key states and requests time-indexed. Theorem~\ref{thm:tombstone} should
  then extend to ``forgetting a revocation is unsafe'', and the one-bit bound grows to cover each
  validity interval.
\item \emph{Derived values.} An amount computed from several sources (a sum, a conversion)
  should carry the meet of their labels. Show that the meet is the only safe label for a derived
  value, and bound what must be kept to compute it.
\item \emph{Withdrawal.} A customer who asks and then says ``cancel that'' withdraws a request.
  Neither $\Perm$ nor the gate models this: a later refusal leaves the earlier request
  outstanding. A withdrawal event resets $\rho_a$ to $\mathsf{absent}$. Faithfulness
  then covers refusals too, and $R$ misreading a refusal becomes a source of ASR.
\item \emph{Approvals in the continuation.} Theorem~\ref{thm:exact} leaves interface
  approvals out. With them, $\mathsf{blocked}$ is no longer absorbing, and the classes may
  change.
\item \emph{Generality.} The results are stated for one authority semantics. The exact
  characterisation (Theorem~\ref{thm:exact}) and the safe-loss theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:safe})
  should hold for any semantics built from monotone accumulation with conflicts, spending and
  resets. State that class and prove them once for it.
\item \emph{Bounds from measured error rates.} Instantiate
  $\mathrm{ASR} \le \Pr[\text{not faithful or not complete}]$ with separately measured error rates
  of $Q$, $R$, $L$ and $E$ on each model, and compare the bound with the measured ASR.
\item \emph{Delegation.} Several principals (a customer and a representative) turn $\Own$ into a
  relation between principals and actions; the ceiling then depends on the principal, not only on
  the channel.
\end{enumerate}
